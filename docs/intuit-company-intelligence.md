## Executive Summary

Intuit is a global financial technology platform serving over 100 million customers across its flagship products — TurboTax, Credit Karma, QuickBooks, and Mailchimp. The company's overarching mission is to **power prosperity around the world** by solving the most important financial problems for consumers and small businesses.

Intuit's current strategic direction is anchored in becoming an **AI-driven expert platform**, with significant investment in generative AI (Intuit Assist) embedded across its product suite. Leadership under CEO **Sasan Goodarzi** continues to emphasize the "Big Bets" framework — a set of long-term strategic priorities designed to create durable competitive advantage and accelerate growth.

**2026 is declared a "pivotal, consequential year."** Goodarzi has narrowed the company's focus to three priorities: **done-for-you experiences** (automating work customers would otherwise do themselves); **mid-market business customers**; and **emphasising money benefits** in everything Intuit delivers. His thesis is that while the market is currently rewarding chips, energy, and infrastructure, value will ultimately shift to companies like Intuit that own **proprietary customer data** and apply AI to solve real problems — making data "the most important moat" in the AI era.

Key themes shaping the company today:
- **Proprietary data as the core competitive moat** — LLMs are commodities; Intuit's 100M customer dataset is not
- **Done-for-you experiences** as the primary product direction across all BUs
- **Deep focus on the mid-market SMB segment** in QuickBooks
- **Money benefits framing** — it's not about taxes, it's about the refund; not about accounting, it's about cash flow
- **3X AI-Native Builder Initiative** — agent-driven development as the company's default operating model
- **"Day one" and "burning platform" culture** — permanent urgency, perpetual self-questioning baked into leadership rhythm
